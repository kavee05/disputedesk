Judge guide for the PayPal AI Hackathon

A small seller gets a PayPal dispute on a parcel the courier marked as delivered. Winning means an hour of digging through the courier site, PayPal and old emails, then writing a formal reply before a deadline. For a $30 order most sellers simply refund. DisputeDesk does the digging and the writing, shows its reasoning, and lets the seller decide with one click. Everything runs on the PayPal Sandbox, so no real money moves.

Try it in two minutes

  1. Open the live demo. The free server may take up to a minute to wake if nobody has visited for a while.
  2. Open Pandan leaf placemats on the Disputes page and press Run the agent. Gemini answers in about 15 seconds.
  3. Read the four steps. This buyer message tries to give the AI orders, and the agent flags it and argues from the delivery record anyway.
  4. Edit the draft if you like, then press Approve and submit evidence. The case now says Processing, never a guessed result.
  5. About 20 seconds later a decision panel appears on its own. Press Decide for seller and the case turns to Won.
  6. Look at Impact, Policy and the Audit log. Demo cases puts the starting data back whenever you want.

No login is needed. The hosted copy is in demo mode with clearly labelled simulated PayPal data and real Google Gemini reasoning. The README explains how to run it against the real PayPal Sandbox with your own keys.

How the agent works

1

Case Analyst

Google Gemini

Reads the numbered evidence from PayPal, estimates the chance of winning, and recommends fight, refund or a human decision.

2

Evidence Writer

Google Gemini

Drafts the reply and cites evidence for every claim. Code then flags any claim that has nothing behind it.

3

Policy Gate

Plain code, no AI

Checks the seller's limits on amount, confidence and dispute reason. It allows the step, asks the seller, or blocks it.

4

Executor

PayPal Disputes API

Reads the dispute again, sends only allowlisted actions PayPal still offers, and stores PayPal's answer exactly as received.

Before deciding, the app weighs the two options with the seller's own numbers. If p is the chance of winning, A the disputed amount and T the cost of the seller's review time, refunding costs A while fighting costs (1 − p) × A + T on average. The case page draws this as a balance scale.

What the sandbox taught us

Nothing settles instantly

Every action returns success at once, then the dispute sits in review for a few minutes. The app shows Processing at PayPal and checks again instead of assuming the result.

The refund is the real record

On the candle case the refund was complete two minutes before the dispute said so. The app now trusts the refund itself, and only that, before it shows Refunded.

Typed evidence was refused

The sandbox rejected tracking sent as proof of fulfilment, even though it had asked for it. Evidence of type Other works, so the tracking details travel in the notes.

The decision tool takes its time

The sandbox adjudication option appeared 18 to 28 minutes after evidence went in. Until then a case honestly reads Under review at PayPal.

Rules the code enforces

Status comes from PayPal

The AI can recommend and draft. It can never mark a case won or refunded.

Buyer text is never an instruction

Messages are wrapped as untrusted claims, and any attempt to steer the agent sends the case to the seller.

Personal data stays home

Names, emails, phones and card numbers are removed before the model sees anything. Tracking numbers stay, because they are the evidence.

The seller stays in charge

The agent only acts alone on small, clear cases, never on unauthorised payment claims, and every draft can be edited first.

Secrets stay on the server

Keys live in environment variables, never enter a prompt, and are stripped from the audit log.

Simulation never poses as PayPal

In demo mode every message says Simulated PayPal. A test checks that this can never slip.
